# The `globals` section

> **For agentic workers:** one dispatch per batch. Read the manual first — this plan carries
> anchors and open questions, never draft prose ([ADR 0010](../adr/0010-entries-are-written-from-the-manual.md)).

**Goal:** author the 32 entries in `content/docs/standard-library/globals/`, after `string`,
`table` and `math`.

**Why this section next.** Three reasons, in order of weight.

It is the section everything else already links into. `table`'s `meta.json` lists eight
globals under _Related globals_; `table.unpack` links to `globals/unpack`, which is a stub
whose version facts are *already settled* and recorded in
`.superpowers/sdd/2026-08-05-table-and-math/task-T3-report.md`. `tests/content/examples.test.ts`
enforces that no example shadows one of the 31 documented globals — the guard has been
policing a list of pages that do not exist yet.

It exercises every fork the slice built, at once. Two constants (`_G`, `_VERSION`), a
five-symbol removed family, a section overview, and twenty-four ordinary functions. If the
three forks are sound, this is where that shows.

It is the section a beginner actually reads. `print`, `type`, `pairs`, `pcall` and
`tostring` are the first five names anyone meets.

## What is different here

**The manual files these in a different place.** They are §5.1 in 5.1 and §6.1 from 5.2 —
"Basic Functions" — and 5.5 inserted a section that pushes the numbering. The anchors are
`pdf-type`, `pdf-pairs`, `pdf-pcall`, with **no library prefix**. Check every anchor in
every manual; do not assume the `pdf-<library>.<name>` shape holds here, because it does not.

**Several of these are language features wearing a function's clothes.** `pairs` consults
`__pairs` on the versions that have it; `tostring` consults `__tostring` and `__name`;
`getmetatable` consults `__metatable`; `error` interacts with message handlers and levels.
Each of those metamethods has its own arrival version. The rule from `string`'s slice
applies: record a fact where the manual documents it for *this* symbol, not where it is a
consequence of a language-wide rule.

**The removed family is bigger and messier than `math`'s.** `getfenv`, `setfenv`, `unpack`,
`loadstring` and `module` all leave, and they do not leave together or for the same reason.
`math` settled the ruling that decides these — *a version has the symbol iff that version's
manual asserts its existence; deprecation asserts existence, silence does not* — and it was
upheld on review. **Apply that ruling; do not re-derive it.** But re-derive every *number*
per symbol, and check `luaconf.h` and the shipped makefile as well as the manual, because
the two axes disagree here as they did for `math`.

**`_ENV` is not in this section** and is not a global. Do not author it. Where `getfenv`
and `setfenv` need to explain what replaced them, link rather than explain.

### G9 inherits settled research

`.superpowers/sdd/2026-08-05-table-and-math/task-T3-report.md` records, with sources, that
the bare `unpack` global exists in 5.1 only *by the manual*, and that a **stock Lua 5.2 build
ships it anyway** via `LUA_COMPAT_UNPACK` under `-DLUA_COMPAT_ALL`. A batch asserted the
opposite and it was a Critical. The `globals/unpack` dataset wants `version_added: "5.1"`,
`version_removed: "5.2"` under the ruling above — but read the report before writing, and
note the build-axis residual in prose only if a manual mentions it, which none does.

### G8 is not a normal constant batch

`_G` and `_VERSION` take the constant fork, and `_VERSION`'s value differs per version —
which is exactly the thing prose may not carry. It is a `Value` section whose content *is* a
version fact. Decide how, and record it; the constant fork has no `<Errors>` and therefore
no licensed `<Since>`, so a `changed_in` is the instrument.

`collectgarbage` is the hardest single entry in the section. Its option strings appear,
disappear and change meaning across five versions, and the generational collector arrives.
Establish which options exist in which version before writing a word of prose.

`npm run types:check`, `npm run build`. Every example executed and its expected output

**Additionally, every batch runs the absolutist sweep before committing** — grep for every,
always, never, all, any, cannot, none, no, only, **including inside `<Return>`, `<Param>`,
`<Errors>` and `<Gotcha title>` bodies**. Every batch in the previous slice shipped at least
one defect of that shape, and two batches' own sweeps missed ones their reviewers found. It
is the single highest-yield check available.
