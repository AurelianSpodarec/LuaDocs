Rounding a number to a whole one, square roots and logarithms, trigonometry, the largest or
smallest of a set of values, pseudo-random draws, and a handful of numeric limits worth
having a name for: this is the library that carries them. Its functions and its constants
both live in the table `math`.

Angles here are in radians. The trigonometric functions read their argument as radians and
the inverse ones answer in radians, and converting to and from degrees is what
[`math.rad()`](/docs/standard-library/math/rad) and
[`math.deg()`](/docs/standard-library/math/deg) are for.

Lua's `number` type has two ways of holding a value inside it — as an *integer* and as a
*float* — and which of the two a call here hands back is rarely that function's own choice.
A few patterns run through the library, and most of these functions fall into one of them
rather than deciding for themselves. Whether the distinction exists at all varies by Lua line
rather than by function: where Lua keeps none, every number is a float and there is nothing
for those patterns to separate.
[`math.floor()`](/docs/standard-library/math/floor#which-subtype-comes-back) sets them out in
one place, and the entries that follow one link there rather than restating it.

<RunnableExample code={`local room_width_metres = 4.2\nlocal room_length_metres = 3.6\nlocal floor_area = room_width_metres * room_length_metres\nlocal packs_needed = math.ceil(floor_area / 2.5)\nprint(floor_area)\nprint(packs_needed)\nprint(math.max(packs_needed, 8))\n-- Expected output:\n-- 15.12\n-- 7\n-- 8`} />

The area is unmistakably a float. The two numbers under it are whole ones, and whether Lua is
holding *those* as integers or as floats is not something the printed form tells you — which
is the question the paragraph above points at, and the first thing about this library worth
reading.

<Note>
The functions that answer with a float whatever went in — the logarithms, the exponentials,
the trigonometry — compute in floating-point arithmetic, where a value that ought to come out
exact usually lands a hair away from it. That is why `math.sin()` of a half-turn is a very
small number rather than zero, and why comparing one of these results with `==` tends to
disappoint.
[`math.sin()`](/docs/standard-library/math/sin#why-the-familiar-values-are-not-exact) works
through where the error comes from, and comparing against a tolerance instead is the usual
answer.
</Note>

Which symbols this table holds has moved about a good deal, including one pair that left and
later came back. Each entry's own version support says where its line falls, and the sidebar
dims what your selected version does not have.

## Rounding and remainders

Getting from a number with a fraction on it to a whole one, and recovering what a division
leaves behind.

- [`math.floor()`](/docs/standard-library/math/floor) — a number rounded down to the nearest whole number
- [`math.ceil()`](/docs/standard-library/math/ceil) — a number rounded up to the nearest whole number instead
- [`math.fmod()`](/docs/standard-library/math/fmod) — the remainder of a division whose quotient is rounded toward zero

## Comparing and measuring

How far a number sits from zero, which of several values wins, and one comparison that reads
a sign bit as part of the value — which needs integers to be a kind of number of their own.

- [`math.abs()`](/docs/standard-library/math/abs) — a number with its sign dropped, which is its distance from zero for all but one integer
- [`math.max()`](/docs/standard-library/math/max) — the largest of the values handed to it
- [`math.min()`](/docs/standard-library/math/min) — the smallest of the values handed to it
- [`math.ult()`](/docs/standard-library/math/ult) — two integers compared as unsigned, so the sign bit counts as value rather than as sign

## Powers, roots and logarithms

Growth in one direction and back again, with the square root standing beside them.

- [`math.sqrt()`](/docs/standard-library/math/sqrt) — the square root of a number
- [`math.exp()`](/docs/standard-library/math/exp) — e, the base of natural logarithms, raised to a power
- [`math.log()`](/docs/standard-library/math/log) — the logarithm of a number, natural, or in a base you name where it reads one

## Angles and trigonometry

The three ratios, the three inverses that recover an angle from a ratio, and the pair that
converts between the two units an angle is written in.

- [`math.sin()`](/docs/standard-library/math/sin) — the sine of an angle measured in radians
- [`math.cos()`](/docs/standard-library/math/cos) — the cosine of an angle measured in radians
- [`math.tan()`](/docs/standard-library/math/tan) — the tangent of an angle measured in radians
- [`math.asin()`](/docs/standard-library/math/asin) — the angle, in radians, whose sine you already have
- [`math.acos()`](/docs/standard-library/math/acos) — the angle, in radians, whose cosine you already have
- [`math.atan()`](/docs/standard-library/math/atan) — the angle a slope describes, or the one a rise and a run describe together, where it reads both
- [`math.rad()`](/docs/standard-library/math/rad) — an angle converted from degrees to radians
- [`math.deg()`](/docs/standard-library/math/deg) — an angle converted from radians to degrees

## Splitting a number, and asking what kind it is

What a number is made of — a whole part and a fraction, or a fraction and a power of two —
and which of the two ways of holding a number it is being held by. That last question can
only be asked where Lua tells the two apart, so the two entries that ask it are there
wherever the distinction is.

- [`math.modf()`](/docs/standard-library/math/modf) — a number split into its whole part and its fraction
- [`math.frexp()`](/docs/standard-library/math/frexp) — a number split into a fraction and a power of two, on the lines that have it
- [`math.ldexp()`](/docs/standard-library/math/ldexp) — a number scaled by a power of two in one exact step, which is the way back from the two values `math.frexp()` hands out, on the lines that have it
- [`math.tointeger()`](/docs/standard-library/math/tointeger) — the integer a value names exactly, or `nil` when it names none
- [`math.type()`](/docs/standard-library/math/type) — whether a number is held as an integer or as a float, and `nil` for anything that is not a number

## Random numbers

One function draws the next number from the generator; the other decides which sequence it is
drawing from, which is what makes a run repeatable.

- [`math.random()`](/docs/standard-library/math/random) — the next pseudo-random number: a fraction below one, or a whole number from a range
- [`math.randomseed()`](/docs/standard-library/math/randomseed) — the state that generator draws from, set so a sequence can be asked for again

## Constants

Four values rather than four calls — read as fields, never called. Two of them name the ends
of the integer range, and so are here wherever an integer is a kind of number of its own.

- [`math.pi`](/docs/standard-library/math/pi) — the closest float there is to the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter
- [`math.huge`](/docs/standard-library/math/huge) — a float above every finite number, and where a float computation lands when it overflows
- [`math.maxinteger`](/docs/standard-library/math/maxinteger) — the largest value an integer can hold, and the edge counting wraps around at
- [`math.mininteger`](/docs/standard-library/math/mininteger) — the smallest value an integer can hold, one step further from zero than the largest

## Functions best written another way

Six the library has carried at one time or another and newer Lua no longer does. They are
listed because reading old code means meeting them; each entry says what to write in its
place, and how exact the swap is.

- [`math.pow()`](/docs/standard-library/math/pow) — one number raised to the power of another, which the `^` operator does
- [`math.log10()`](/docs/standard-library/math/log10) — a base-ten logarithm, which `math.log()` gives when you name the base — with one thing to check first
- [`math.atan2()`](/docs/standard-library/math/atan2) — an angle from a rise and a run, which `math.atan()` takes both of once it is reading both
- [`math.cosh()`](/docs/standard-library/math/cosh) — the hyperbolic cosine, and how to write one out of `math.exp()`
- [`math.sinh()`](/docs/standard-library/math/sinh) — the hyperbolic sine, written the same way but with one step that costs precision
- [`math.tanh()`](/docs/standard-library/math/tanh) — the hyperbolic tangent, where dividing the two above is the form to avoid
